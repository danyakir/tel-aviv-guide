# Standing rules for every weekend guide

These rules apply to every future search and scheduled Wednesday run.

1. Search independent event publishers and official venue calendars in Hebrew and English. Aim for at least three independent website domains with matching, dated events. Sections of the same website count as one source.
2. Prefer direct venue or organizer event pages when the same event appears on an aggregator. Keep the actual event URL, and display its destination domain and source.
3. Aim for about 30 matching events. Balance independent domains as a preference, never a hard percentage that deletes good events or shrinks a full guide. Reserve representation for available independent websites, then fill remaining spaces with relevant events. Explicitly disclose limited coverage if fewer than three websites return matches. Never invent events to meet a quota.
4. Include a price field on every event. Extract advertised ticket prices from the event page or structured ticket offers. Preserve ranges and conditions. If unavailable, say “Price not published — check event page”. Never infer free admission from a missing price or from a venue's usual policy.
5. Require an actual event date within Thursday evening through Saturday night. A general nightlife article or a venue mention is not proof of a scheduled event.
6. Remove duplicates, retain useful prices from duplicate listings, and mark cross-source verification only when independent domains confirm the event.
7. Show one bordered card per event, grouped chronologically by day and time. Include title, date/time, location, price, short description when available, source, and event link. List only sources actually represented in the guide footer.
8. Log the number of matching events each collector returns. Website failures must not fabricate fallback events. Report limited source coverage clearly.
9. The guide is primarily for techno, disco, house, electronic parties, and raves, plus young alternative local culture. Search Teder and similar bars, venues and areas, including Phi Garden, FOMA, Hive, Nuweiba, Radio EPGB, Kuli Alma, Room 48, Gagarin and Levontin. Search independent nightlife discovery sites as well as venue calendars, and follow their organizer/ticket links. Aim for roughly 20 nightlife events, 7 relevant shows/films, and 3 young daytime/community events. These are flexible targets; relevance matters more than filling any quota. Do not label a general mainstream party as techno without supporting event information.
10. Rebuild from live listings for the upcoming weekend on every run. Never reuse a previous preview as the next guide, carry over a past party, or assume a recurring event is happening without a dated occurrence. Dates and years must follow the requested weekend, including year changes.
11. Within the preferred music styles, favor a variety of venues, organizers, and genres, plus a spread across Thursday, Friday, and Saturday. Teder and the named venues are starting points, not a fixed set of events or an exclusive venue list. Avoid letting one busy venue crowd out equally relevant alternatives; use soft preferences rather than excluding good events to satisfy a quota.
12. Keep the same readable card layout and explicit price/source fields every week. Use only the events and facts available for that weekend. When a source or category is sparse, fill from the remaining relevant listings and report limitations honestly.
13. Prioritize evening events over morning and daytime events, especially Friday evening. Favor Friday evening until about eight options are represented when suitable listings exist. Choose evening parties and shows first within their categories, keep a smaller selection of free daytime activities, and fill unused slots from daytime listings only when evening supply is limited. Keep actual start times and chronological grouping; never move an afternoon event into the evening section just to improve the counts.
14. Do not include folk dancing / הרקדה / ריקודי עם, senior activities, generic country-club walks, singalongs, family activities, or unrelated municipal filler. Free admission or municipal sponsorship alone is not a reason to include an event. Municipal picks must have relevant music, arts, design, street culture, young creative/community, or local-bar context. Favor alternative/indie/rock and local-scene shows over generic mainstream entertainment.
15. Restaurant openings belong only when there is an actual dated opening party, launch, pop-up, tasting or similar event during the weekend. Never include a generic new-restaurant article or monthly roundup as an event.
16. Audit Friday-night coverage before trusting a sparse result. Consult multiple nightlife calendars and direct event/organizer pages; a weak result from one collector is not evidence that Friday has few parties. Count parties separately from shows when reporting how many Friday-night parties were found.

The weekly scheduler uses `fetch_weekend_events` and `build_message`, which enforce the selection, price display, provenance, and layout rules on every run. When adding or repairing collectors, preserve these rules and their regression tests.
